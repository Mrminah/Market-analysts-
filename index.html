Trading styles explained

Scalper

Holds trades from seconds to about an hour and takes many small trades a day. Uses the 1H chart for direction and the 5M or 15M chart for entries. It needs constant attention, and spreads eat a large share of small profits.

Day trader

Opens and closes trades within the same day and does not hold overnight. Takes a few trades a day using the 4H and 1H charts for direction and the 15M chart for entries. Targets are sized to fit inside the day’s range.

Swing trader

Holds trades for several days up to a few weeks. Takes fewer trades with wider stops and smaller lot sizes, using the weekly and daily charts for direction and the 4H chart for entries. Overnight and weekend gaps are the main risk.

Position and fundamental trader

Holds trades for weeks or months and decides mainly from the economy: interest rates, inflation, jobs data and central bank decisions, not chart patterns. This site uses chart analysis only and does not read fundamentals. Big news releases can still move price against any style, so be careful around them.

How this site labels signals

By how much of the day’s average range the first target needs. Under 25% is a Scalp, 25% to 100% is a Day trade, and over 100% is a Swing. Use the Mode setting to see only the style you trade.
